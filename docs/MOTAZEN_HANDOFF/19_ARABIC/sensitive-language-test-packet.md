# Sensitive Arabic language test packet

## Status

Protocol draft only. No participant results exist. Do not mark terminology approved until attributable Egyptian language reviewers and diverse adult participants complete the tests with consent and safety protections.

## Audiences to include

Recruit across age, education and literacy, region and urban/rural setting, gender, disability and assistive technology, religion and non-religion, formal/informal/unpaid work, caregiving, shared-device use, and exposure to healthcare or mental-health terminology. Do not treat any group as representative of Egypt.

## Tasks for every sensitive term

1. Read or listen to the MSA term and explain it back without seeing the definition.
2. Read or listen to the Egyptian explanation and identify what became clearer or more confusing.
3. Choose which prohibited interpretation the text successfully rules out.
4. Apply the term to a low-risk vignette and explain where it should not be used.
5. Mark language that sounds clinical, preachy, foreign, class-specific, childish, gendered, blaming, stigmatizing, unsafe, or too informal.
6. Test a no-writing route and a screen-reader/audio route.
7. Ask whether the phrase could create conflict or retaliation if visible on a shared device.

## Critical scenario set

- Responsibility after structural or interpersonal harm without victim blaming.
- A family or workplace boundary under unequal power without prescribing confrontation.
- Immediate danger with no verified service number.
- Persistent attention difficulty without self-diagnosis or dismissal.
- Sleep and movement language without personal medical prescription.
- Low energy without “laziness,” positivity pressure, or causal inference.
- Religious and non-religious value language without one moral answer key.
- No-save privacy wording on a shared or monitored device.

## Decision record

For each term record participant group, route used, comprehension, misinterpretation, emotional or safety concern, proposed wording, disagreement, reviewer decision, and unresolved minority view. Aggregate counts never replace verbatim concern review. A severe safety misunderstanding blocks publication even if most participants understood the wording.
