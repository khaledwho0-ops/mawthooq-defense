# Egyptian society and language critical review — pilot Days 001–020

**Reviewer type:** internal AI-assisted critical lens, not Egyptian participatory research and not cultural approval.  
**Decision:** `REVISE_INTERNAL / EGYPTIAN_USER_RESEARCH_REQUIRED`.

## What was directly checked

Every Egyptian-context example and special-context adaptation was reviewed for class, work, commuting, caregiving, housing, gender and family power, disability, informal work, religion and non-religion, privacy, and urban assumptions. Source records were checked for direct-Egypt evidence and its sampling limits.

## Strengths observed

- Examples recognize commuting, shared housing, unpaid care, informal work, shift work, heat, device dependence, and economic constraint.
- Values content explicitly permits religious and non-religious frameworks and refuses one ideological answer key.
- Responsibility language distinguishes agency from structural blame; relationship practices are conditional on safety and power.
- The text does not present one Egyptian survey, clinic, student sample, or household study as representative of all adults.

## Blocking findings

1. `PILOT-EGY-001 — HIGH`: most lesson prose is Modern Standard Arabic with occasional Egyptian wording. The balance between clarity, dignity, accessibility, and natural Egyptian speech has not been tested across education, region, age, and disability.
2. `PILOT-EGY-002 — HIGH`: direct Egyptian evidence remains sparse and heterogeneous. Examples are informed design hypotheses, not evidence that the practices fit Egyptian life.
3. `PILOT-EGY-003 — HIGH`: gendered power, dependence, housing, harassment, family authority, and retaliation need scenario-based review with participants who face those conditions; generic “if safe” wording is necessary but insufficient.
4. `PILOT-EGY-004 — MEDIUM`: examples lean toward literate smartphone users able to write privately. Voice, icon, no-storage, and supported-reading paths need user testing rather than translation alone.

## Required internal revisions

- Create a terminology registry with preferred MSA term, optional Egyptian explanation, prohibited stigmatizing alternatives, audience notes, and professional/user-review state.
- Create a line-by-line language test packet for the most sensitive terms: dignity, laziness, balance, diagnosis, help, danger, coercion, boundaries, sleep, and attention.
- Keep all local cultural claims marked as hypotheses until participatory sessions are completed and documented.

## Human review questions

- Which phrases sound clinical, preachy, foreign, class-specific, gendered, or shaming?
- Can low-literacy and screen-reader users understand the same boundary without losing nuance?
- When is not writing or not involving family the safest culturally realistic route?

No cultural or language approval is granted.
