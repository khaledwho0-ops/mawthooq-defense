# Behavioral-science and curriculum critical review — pilot Days 001–020

**Reviewer type:** internal AI-assisted critical lens, not an independent behavioral scientist or curriculum professional.  
**Decision:** `REVISE_INTERNAL / HUMAN_REVIEW_PENDING`.

## What was directly checked

The review compared every title, objective, prerequisite, capability, claim, pattern, practice family, full/standard/minimum mode, failure response, tomorrow cue, and real-world mission against the audited outline. Exact reuse of twelve core content fields was also tested automatically.

## Strengths observed

- The sequence progresses coherently: orientation and description, context and dignity, values and roles, problem framing and pattern literacy, responsibility, then retrieval; Phase 2 moves through energy, sleep, movement, attention, digital friction, realistic time, minimum mode, and integration.
- Days 10 and 20 use low-stakes retrieval and explicitly separate understanding, attempt, effect, and harm.
- Every practice has alternatives and a non-punitive failure response. Minimum mode is a complete route rather than a teaser for more work.
- Core definitions, examples, actions, practices, missions, and reflections are not copied between written days.

## Blocking findings

1. `PILOT-CURR-001 — HIGH`: the reading burden is excessive. Forty-four fields are a content contract, not a requirement to display every field serially. A progressive-disclosure presentation model and comprehension test are required.
2. `PILOT-CURR-002 — HIGH`: the first nine days are concept-dense. Human testing must verify recall of the distinctions and whether Day 10 comes soon enough; the outline alone cannot prove cognitive-load safety.
3. `PILOT-CURR-003 — MEDIUM`: some days offer many optional tasks across action, practice, experiment, mission, and relationship fields. The UI must identify one primary practice and collapse alternatives to prevent perceived homework accumulation.
4. `PILOT-CURR-004 — MEDIUM`: tomorrow notices support continuity but could feel like persistent monitoring. They must remain optional, non-notifying by default, and absent from streak logic.

## Required internal revisions

- Add a presentation contract that shows objective, core explanation, one primary practice, safety, and completion first; other fields are optional layers.
- Define a per-day primary practice and prohibit the UI from presenting every practice field as mandatory.
- Add pilot measures for explanation-back, delayed retrieval, attempted behavior, burden, confusion, and negative outcome—not satisfaction alone.

## Human review questions

- Can diverse adult readers explain the five Day 10 distinctions without reopening every lesson?
- Do full, standard, and minimum modes feel equally legitimate?
- Which fields should be audio, expandable, or omitted from the default view?

No curriculum approval is granted.
